Fly Terrarium

A fly in a Petri dish. Its decisions come from a model of its whole nervous system, built from the connectome: a female from FlyWire (138,639 neurons, brain only) or a male from the MaleCNS (165,122 neurons, brain and nerve cord). Put something next to it and watch which neurons answer.

What is real here and what is made up

Real. The brain is the model of Shiu et al. (Nature, 2024): each neuron integrates charge and fires, each synapse excites or inhibits according to its neurotransmitter. It runs right in the browser and matches our Python version (brain.py, checked against the original in Brian2): correlation of firing rates 0.9996 on FlyWire and 0.9998 on the MaleCNS. Which output neurons answer a stimulus is decided by the wiring alone.

  • Sugar and bitter reach the taste neurons of the proboscis; the output is the proboscis motor neuron MN9. Bitter suppresses the answer to sugar, as in the paper.
  • A puff bends the antennae: the mechanoreceptors of Johnston's organ (JO-CE, JO-F); the output is aDN1/aDN2, the command to clean the antennae. The closer to the fly you puff, the stronger: from 200 Hz right at the fly to zero at 14 mm.
  • The shadow drives the looming detectors LC4, LPLC2, LC16 of each eye. Outputs: the giant fibre DNp01 (escape), DNa01/DNa02 right minus left (turning), MDN (backing up).

Female and male. These are two different flies, scanned by different labs. They get the same stimuli but answer differently:

  • FlyWire ends at the neck and has no jump motor neuron, so the female jumps by a made-up rule, "a giant fibre spike = a jump". The male has a nerve cord, and its jump is triggered by the motor neuron TTMn: the command goes from the eyes to the muscle through the wiring.
  • To make the female clean her antennae you have to puff almost right at her (from 150 Hz), and only her left antenna answers: that is how the wiring is, not a bug of the page. The male grooms even after a distant puff (from 50 Hz), with both antennae, but the right one also twitches the proboscis.
  • In the male, sugar extends the proboscis only through the left MN9; the right one stays silent.
  • The male brain is bistable: a puff right at the fly (200 Hz) starts a wave in about 5,600 neurons (Kenyon cells of the mushroom bodies, dopaminergic PAM neurons, the central complex). It goes round in circles until you calm it. In a living fly fatigue and inhibition, which the model lacks, would put it out. While it lasts, the browser computes the brain 10–25 times slower than real time.
  • A male synapse weighs 1.8 times less: on the same neurons the MaleCNS finds 1.8 times as many synapses as FlyWire, and the weight in the model was fitted to FlyWire.

Made up. The stroll while the brain is silent (the model has no spontaneous activity), the walls of the dish, the strength of the stimuli in hertz, the eye as a formula of how fast the shadow grows, turning spike rates into movements, the body animation. In the Python version of the project the fly has a real physical body and eyes of 721 facets.

Control. "Shuffle the wiring" leaves each neuron its synapses but sends them to random neurons. If the fly still fed and escaped, the connectome would not be the reason.
